How to play

  1. The race. Each side has fifteen checkers. Bring all of yours into your home board, the bottom right quarter, and then bear them off. Yours move round the board anticlockwise: along the top from the right, down the left, and along the bottom to the right.
  2. Moving. Roll two dice and move a checker the number shown on each; the same checker may move twice. Doubles are played four times. A checker may stop on any point not held by two or more of the other side's.
  3. Using the dice. Use both dice whenever you can. If only one can be used, it must be the larger. If neither can, press Done.
  4. Hitting. A lone checker that is landed on goes to the bar. A checker on the bar must come back in, in the other side's home board, before anything else moves.
  5. Bearing off. Once all your checkers are home, a die takes off a checker from the point it shows, or from the highest point if none is higher. The first side to bear off all fifteen wins.
  6. Scoring. A win is a single game, worth 1 point. If the loser has borne off none, it is a gammon, worth 2; and if the loser still has a checker on the bar or in the winner's home board, a backgammon, worth 3. The first side to reach the match length wins the match.
  7. The doubling cube. Before rolling you may double the stakes. The other side takes, and owns the cube, so only it may double next; or passes, and loses the game at the stakes before the double. In the Crawford game, the one after a side first comes within a point of winning the match, nobody may double.
  8. Notation. 13/7 moves a checker from point 13 to point 7, counting from the mover's side; bar and off mean the bar and bearing off, and * marks a hit. The points are numbered for the side to play.

Tap a checker, and the places it may go light up; tap one to move there, or drag the checker. Undo takes back moves until you press Done. Tap anywhere to skip an animation or a celebration. On a keyboard: the arrow keys and Space move checkers, Enter presses the main button, U undoes, D doubles, H asks for a hint and M mutes the sound.
